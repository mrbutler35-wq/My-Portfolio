About / My Journey

From the shop floor to intelligent systems.

Technology matters when it works for the people depending on it.

My path into AI didn't start in a computer science classroom. It started on the manufacturing floor. For years, I worked where engineering decisions become real products: programming CNC equipment, working with engineering drawings and CAD data, managing bills of material, supporting production, and maintaining critical product information across SAP ERP and Siemens Teamcenter.

As my career progressed, I moved deeper into quality, enterprise systems, process improvement, and customer-facing technical work. I investigated failures, traced materials and documentation, and worked across engineering and operations. I saw how disconnected data and inefficient processes could affect an entire organization. At Mettler-Toledo, that problem-solving mindset contributed to a Lean Six Sigma / Kaizen initiative that helped consolidate production from three shifts to one while increasing throughput by approximately 40%.

Alongside engineering and operations, customer-facing work—including residential real estate—strengthened how I discover what people need, communicate with stakeholders, weigh business considerations, and turn requirements into action. That experience complements my technical work: building a useful solution starts with understanding the people and problem it needs to serve.

Eventually, I realized I didn't just want to work inside these systems—I wanted to build better ones. That led me into software, data, artificial intelligence, and machine learning: Python, SQL, cloud technologies, APIs, Generative AI, RAG, and MLOps. I completed TripleTen's AI & Machine Learning Engineering program while building increasingly production-oriented AI, data, and software projects.

Today, those two sides of my career come together. At Manzi Metals, I began developing ForgeIQ after seeing how valuable operational information could become fragmented across sales, purchasing, receiving, delivery, and quality. I approached it as an engineering problem: use Python, SQL, data validation, reconciliation, relational matching, and ML-ready pipelines to connect enterprise information and create a trustworthy foundation for operational intelligence.

My work now spans machine learning systems, Generative AI applications, REST APIs, computer vision, RAG workflows, containerized applications, cloud deployment, CI/CD, model monitoring, and end-to-end MLOps. But the technology isn't the whole story. I've worked alongside operators, engineers, quality teams, suppliers, customers, sales teams, and enterprise systems. I understand traceability, data integrity, production constraints, customer requirements, and why a solution has to work operationally—not just technically.

RAG · Knowledge assistant

ForgeRAG

A retrieval-augmented question-answering assistant for an industrial metal-supply knowledge base. It chunks documents, generates embeddings, retrieves relevant context from ChromaDB, and uses extractive question answering to produce grounded responses with source attribution.

Retrieval and answer confidence are combined as response gates; when confidence is insufficient, the assistant returns a fallback rather than forcing an answer. The repository also includes a structured evaluation workflow for different question categories.

Machine learning · Diabetes risk

Pima Indians Diabetes Database

An end-to-end capstone combining a reproducible tabular ML workflow with a natural-language interface. The pipeline treats implausible zero values as missing, applies KNN imputation and feature scaling, runs five MLflow-tracked experiments, selects a model, and evaluates it on a held-out test split.

The interface uses an LLM to extract structured features from user input, runs the selected model, and generates an explanation with medical caveats. The repository states that the output is not a medical diagnosis.

Repository-reported best run: accuracy 0.7403; precision 0.6029; recall 0.7593; F1 0.6721; ROC-AUC 0.8267.
